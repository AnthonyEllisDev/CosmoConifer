Solvane: The Iron Reach

An infinite procedurally generated galaxy you mine, build in, defend, and fly through in a ship you configured yourself.

Ask about the game → Press kit

What it is

Top-down, infinite, procedurally generated. You fly out from your base, scan asteroids to see what they're actually worth before you spend time on them, break the ones that pay, and haul the ore home.

Home is a factory you lay out yourself. Solar arrays make power, conduits move it, and processing turns raw ore into the parts you need. Drones get built to repair the base and help defend it. Turrets cover the approaches, because the galaxy eventually notices you're out there.

Weapons are built rather than bought, so a loadout is a decision instead of a menu. The short version: Factorio and tower defense, in space, in pixel art.

  • An infinite galaxy generated as you fly, with planets and moons on drawn orbital paths
  • Asteroid mining that tells you the yield before you commit to the rock
  • Base building with power generation, conduits, processing and storage
  • Turrets and constructed drones that repair and defend what you've built
  • Ship and weapon configuration from parts you manufacture

Where it came from

No Man's Sky launched in August 2016 and the reception was brutal. On a Discord call I told my friends I could probably make something with that much depth in GameMaker. That was a joke, and then it wasn't.

The early weeks were easy, which is the trap. Ships moved, asteroids broke apart, things exploded. The moment I started building systems that had to talk to each other, the actual size of game development turned up all at once.

Nine years in, the thing I'd point to isn't a feature. It's that I've walked away from this project for months at a time and always come back to it.

The parts that took longest are the ones a screenshot can't show: a chunk system I wrote three times before it held together, save formats, and finding out what nine months away does to a codebase you thought you understood.
